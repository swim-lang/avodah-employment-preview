Before discipline or separation: an employer decision map

Employment decisions become harder when urgency replaces sequence. The most useful pause is time used to identify what decision is being made and what record should support it.

Prepared August 2026Attorney review required before publication

1. Define the decision

Deciding whether conduct occurred, whether a policy applies, whether discipline is warranted and whether employment should continue are different questions. Mixing them can turn an investigation into an argument for an outcome already chosen.

2. Identify legal overlays early

Protected complaints, leave, accommodation, wage concerns, contracts and prior representations may change the analysis. Identify these issues before a final decision.

3. Compare the right examples

Consistency matters, but similarity may depend on role, decision-maker, policy, history, timing and seriousness. The review should explain why examples are comparable or why they are not.

4. Decide whether fact development is sufficient

Some decisions can be made from established records. Others require interviews, document review or an independent investigator. The scope should match the question.

5. Plan communication and the first week after

Decide who will speak, what will be documented, what access will change, how work will be reassigned and what managers should say. A supportable decision can still create avoidable risk if execution is careless.

6. Preserve the reasoning

The record should show the facts relied on, the policy or expectation involved, the decision-maker and the reason for the outcome. Bring counsel in earlier when senior leadership is involved, protected activity may be present or facts are disputed.

This article provides general information only and does not create an attorney-client relationship. Start with the parties, general issue and decision date. Do not send sensitive records until conflicts are cleared.
