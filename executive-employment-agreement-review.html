What an executive agreement should help you understand

An executive agreement is not only a record of compensation. It is a map of the relationship before the role begins, while authority is being exercised and after the relationship ends. A useful review should make that map easier to read.

Prepared August 2026Attorney review required before publication

Start with the whole arrangement

The signed agreement may not contain every important term. Compensation plans, equity documents, bonus policies, confidentiality agreements, restrictive covenants and company policies may be incorporated or delivered separately. Identify every document that controls the relationship, which documents the company can change and what happens if terms conflict.

Understand how value is earned and lost

Base compensation is usually the simplest term. Incentives may depend on performance measures, board discretion, continued employment, a transaction closing or the payment date. Equity may be subject to vesting, repurchase, forfeiture or separate plan rules. A review should identify both the possible value and the conditions that can reduce it.

Define authority as carefully as responsibility

Titles can stay the same while decision rights, reporting lines or resources change. Executives should understand which changes are permitted, which may affect compensation or termination rights, and what process applies if the role becomes materially different.

Read every ending

The consequences may differ if the executive resigns, is terminated without cause, is terminated for cause, leaves for a defined good reason, becomes disabled, dies or remains through a change in control. Each path may change severance, bonus treatment, equity, benefits, notice and transition duties.

Look beyond the last day

Post-employment terms may affect where an executive can work, whom the executive can contact, what information can be used, how the departure is described and whether cooperation is required. These restrictions should be read together.

Decide what is worth negotiating

A practical review ranks terms by consequence, likelihood and negotiability. That keeps the conversation focused on a small number of meaningful outcomes.

This article provides general information only and does not create an attorney-client relationship. A lawyer must clear conflicts before receiving confidential details or advising on an agreement.
