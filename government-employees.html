For Federal and State Employees

Public service has its own employment rules and deadlines.

A grievance, proposed action, EEO matter or administrative appeal can move through a process that differs from a private workplace dispute. Avodah helps federal and state employees identify the forum, the next deadline and the decision that needs attention.

Discuss a Government Employment Matter See the Matter Types
Start With the Notice and the Date

Do not assume that an internal discussion pauses a formal deadline.

The available process may depend on the employer, appointment, personnel action, forum and document received. Preserve the notice and identify every stated response, counseling, grievance or appeal date. An attorney must confirm the applicable procedure for the specific matter.

Government Employment Matters

The right path begins with the employer and the forum.

01

Federal EEO matters

Discrimination, harassment and retaliation concerns that may proceed through an agency's federal-sector EEO process and later review.

02

MSPB and proposed actions

Proposed removals, suspensions and other personnel actions that may involve an agency response, Merit Systems Protection Board review or related strategy.

03

State employee grievances

Workplace disputes, discipline and grievances that may be governed by Virginia agency procedures, employment status and administrative rules.

04

Administrative proceedings

Hearings, appeals and agency processes involving employment, professional standing or another government decision affecting continued work.

05

Discrimination and retaliation disputes

Claims involving protected status, protected activity, whistleblowing or alleged retaliation, including matters that may proceed to litigation.

06

Professional-license intersections

Employment concerns that overlap with licensure, credentialing, reporting duties or proceedings before a Virginia licensing authority.

A Useful First Conversation

Orient the matter before sending the file.

Begin with limited information for a conflict check. Do not send confidential records until Avodah confirms that it can speak with you.

  • The agency or public employer
  • Your role and employment status
  • The general type of action or complaint
  • The date and title of the notice received
  • Every stated response, counseling, grievance or appeal date
  • Whether a union, EEO counselor or agency representative is already involved
