Investigations and Decisions

A credible process supports a credible decision.

When a complaint, conflict or leadership concern requires formal attention, the investigation must be appropriately scoped, fair and useful to the people who must act on it. Avodah helps employers build that process and make the decision that follows.

Discuss an Investigation
When Outside Counsel May Help

Signs the process needs independence.

The allegations involve senior leadership or the internal legal or HR team.
Independence and perceived fairness are important.
The facts cross offices, entities or reporting lines.
Litigation, agency action or public scrutiny is reasonably possible.
The organization needs help defining the question before interviews begin.
Internal teams need advice on the decision after fact development.
The Investigation Sequence

Five steps, agreed before interviews begin.

01

Define the mandate

Clarify who requested the work, what questions will be examined, what authority applies and who receives the result.

02

Preserve and plan

Identify relevant information, witnesses, sequencing, communications and immediate risk.

03

Develop the record

Conduct interviews and review information in a fair, disciplined manner.

04

Assess and communicate

Organize findings according to the agreed mandate and explain what remains uncertain.

05

Support the decision

Help the organization evaluate response, documentation, communication and follow-through.

What Independence Does Not Mean

Independence is clarity, not a promised outcome.

An independent process is not a promise of a particular outcome. It means the mandate, methods and reporting relationship are clear enough for the organization to rely on the work. The correct structure depends on who is involved and why the investigation is being conducted.
