For Executives

Understand the move before you make it.

An executive decision can affect compensation, authority, reputation and the freedom to work next. Avodah helps senior leaders understand the full arrangement, not just the paragraph that first raised concern.

Review an Executive Matter What We Review
The Moments That Deserve a Closer Look

The terms before, during and after the role.

Joining

Offer letters, employment agreements, compensation, equity, authority, reporting lines and conditions that affect the value of the opportunity.

Leading

Changes in responsibility, internal investigations, incentive disputes, protected concerns and decisions that may change the executive's position.

Leaving

Severance, releases, equity treatment, transition duties, communications, references and the restrictions that follow departure.

Competing or beginning again

Noncompete, nonsolicitation, confidentiality, trade-secret and fiduciary questions that may shape the next role or venture.

What a Review Should Produce

Not a redline without a strategy.

A useful review should leave an executive able to answer four questions.

Deliverables may include a prioritized issue list, explanation of leverage and tradeoffs, proposed revisions, negotiation support and a clear record of the final terms. Scope depends on the matter and must be agreed before work begins.

  1. 1What am I receiving, and what could change it?
  2. 2What am I promising while I am in the role?
  3. 3What happens if the relationship ends in different ways?
  4. 4What will I still be allowed and expected to do afterward?
A Discreet Process

Four steps, in confidence.

STEP 1

Conflict and timing

Common Questions

Asked before most reviews begin.

Is a short offer letter enough to review?

+

It can be. Important terms may be incorporated from a plan, handbook, equity document or separate restrictive covenant. A complete review begins by identifying every document that governs the relationship.

Should I wait until I receive the final agreement?

+

Earlier review can be more useful when business terms are still open. If a deadline is close, include it in the first contact.

Can Avodah represent both my employer and me?

+

Not in the same matter. Avodah must identify the parties and complete a conflict analysis before discussing confidential facts.

Does a restriction mean I cannot take the next job?

+

The answer depends on the language, facts and applicable law. Do not rely on a general website explanation for a specific decision.
