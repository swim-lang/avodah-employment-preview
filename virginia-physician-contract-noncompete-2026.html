Physician contracts after Virginia's 2026 noncompete changes

Virginia changed parts of its employment-law framework in 2026, including rules that may affect certain healthcare noncompete arrangements. A statutory change can alter a clause's effect without resolving every practical question in the relationship.

Prepared August 2026Attorney and source review required before publication

Begin with who, when and what kind of agreement

The analysis may depend on the worker's role, organization, agreement date, employment change and current statute. A headline about noncompetes does not answer the question for a particular document.

Read the surrounding restrictions

An agreement may still contain confidentiality, nonsolicitation, notice, repayment, patient-transition, records, intellectual-property and outside-activity terms. The practical effect of leaving often comes from several provisions working together.

Examine the departure economics

Compensation through the last day, incentive calculations, repayment obligations, tail coverage, equity, benefits and notice duties may have more immediate financial impact than the noncompete itself.

Plan for continuity

Physicians and practices may need to address patient communications, credentialing, call coverage, records, pending procedures and professional obligations before the transition becomes public or urgent.

For practices and health systems

Updated forms are only part of compliance. Recruiting, retention, compensation and departure processes should be reviewed when an old restriction can no longer be treated the same way.
