Physician contracts after Virginia's 2026 noncompete changes

Virginia updated its statutory framework for employment restrictions in 2026, including provisions addressing healthcare professionals. A statutory change can affect a clause without resolving every question about compensation, notice, patient continuity, repayment or the transition to a new role.

Working draft updated August 2026Attorney and source review required before publication

Begin with who, when and what kind of agreement

The analysis may depend on the professional's license, the type of organization, the agreement date, later amendments, how employment ends and the language of the current statute. A headline about noncompetes does not answer the question for a particular physician, practice or document.

Start by identifying every agreement that may govern the relationship. The operative terms may appear in an offer letter, employment agreement, ownership document, recruitment agreement, promissory note, policy, compensation plan or later amendment. Determine which document controls and whether an automatic renewal or modification changed the relevant terms.

Do not reduce the review to one restriction

An agreement may contain confidentiality, nonsolicitation, notice, repayment, patient-transition, records, intellectual-property and outside-activity terms. Even if one provision is limited or affected by current law, the practical effect of leaving may come from several other provisions working together.

Read the restrictions as a system. A notice requirement may affect timing. A repayment provision may change the economics. A nonsolicitation clause may affect communications. Credentialing and payer enrollment may influence when a new role can begin. The right question is often what the complete transition requires, not whether one paragraph is enforceable in isolation.

Examine compensation and repayment obligations

Compensation through the last day, productivity calculations, quality incentives, collections, repayment obligations, tail coverage, equity, benefits and notice duties may have more immediate financial impact than the noncompete itself. Recruitment or retention payments may be governed by separate notes or schedules.

Identify when each payment is earned, how it is calculated and what happens after notice or termination. Ask whether the physician can verify the underlying numbers and whether the agreement allows offsets or repayment. A useful review should place possible financial consequences on a timeline.

Plan for patient and practice continuity

Physicians and practices may need to address patient communications, credentialing, call coverage, records, pending procedures, referrals and professional obligations before a transition becomes public or urgent. The agreement may not answer every operational question.

Decide who controls communications, what applicable law or professional duties require and how ongoing care will be protected. Avoid promising patients, staff or a new organization a transition date before licensing, credentialing, notice and contractual questions have been reviewed.

Consider licensing and reporting issues separately

A contract transition may overlap with a peer-review matter, internal investigation, credentialing concern, professional-license inquiry or reporting obligation. Those processes may have different standards, deadlines and confidentiality rules.

Preserve the notice and identify the forum before sending a detailed response. A physician should not assume that resolving the employment issue automatically resolves a licensing, credentialing or administrative matter. Avodah must confirm the scope of any representation separately.

For practices and health systems

Updated forms are only part of compliance. Recruiting, retention, compensation and departure processes should be reviewed when the governing law changes. Leaders should understand which provisions may remain available, which require different drafting and how managers will handle notice, patient communications and continuity.

Consistency also matters. If similar departures receive different treatment, document the operational reason. Coordinate legal, clinical, human-resources and credentialing decisions so that one team does not make a promise another team cannot perform.

Documents to gather after conflicts are cleared

  • The offer letter, employment agreement and all amendments
  • Compensation plans, exhibits and recent calculation statements
  • Recruitment, signing-bonus, retention or repayment documents
  • Restrictive-covenant, confidentiality and nonsolicitation agreements
  • Ownership, equity, malpractice and tail-coverage documents
  • Credentialing, licensing or investigation notices connected to the transition
  • The proposed departure date and the earliest decision deadline

Questions worth answering before a transition

  • Which document and version governs the relationship?
  • What notice is required, and what changes after notice is given?
  • Which compensation remains earned or conditional?
  • What repayment, tail-coverage or ownership obligations may apply?
  • How will patients, records, call coverage and pending care be handled?
  • Does the matter involve a separate licensing, credentialing or investigation process?
  • Which restrictions require current statutory analysis?

Use current law, not memory

Employment restrictions are date-sensitive and fact-specific. Before publication or advice, the reviewing attorney should confirm the current text and effective provisions of the Virginia statute, the professional categories covered, any exceptions and the relationship between the statute and the specific documents.
